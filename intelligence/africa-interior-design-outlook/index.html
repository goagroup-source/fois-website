Nigeria as the anchor market, and the wider case for Sub-Saharan Africa's interior economy — read against urbanization, housing deficit and a hospitality pipeline that is only beginning to build out.

Executive Summary

An interior economy built on scarcity and scale

Africa's interior design sector is a study in contrast: chronic undersupply of formal housing sits alongside some of the fastest urban growth rates on earth, and a small, increasingly professionalised design community serves a market that third-party researchers now size in the tens of billions of dollars. Nigeria — Africa's most populous country and its largest economy by several measures — anchors this outlook, with Lagos functioning as the continent's most-watched design city outside Johannesburg and Nairobi.

Third-party market research puts the African interior design market at roughly $24.3 billion in 2025, expanding toward $35 billion by 2030 at a compound annual growth rate near 6.7%, split primarily across South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya and the "rest of Africa" category. Nigeria's own real estate market — the base on which fit-out, renovation and residential design demand sits — is estimated at $29–32 billion in 2024–2025 and is projected to approach $40 billion by 2030, a trajectory driven by urban migration into Lagos, Abuja and Port Harcourt, a growing services and technology economy, and government-backed urban development policy.

The industry's own trade body, the Interior Designers Association of Nigeria (IDAN), has cited a conservative estimate of over ₦30 billion in annual industry contribution — a figure IDAN's own leadership has publicly argued undercounts the sector by an order of magnitude, pending a fuller GDP-contribution study. What is not in dispute is direction: a housing deficit now projected by Lagos State officials to reach 28 million units nationally is, paradoxically, one of the strongest long-run demand signals for the design and build-out professions FOIS serves.

Methodology

How this outlook was built

This report is FOIS Intelligence's editorial synthesis of publicly available third-party research — market-sizing studies, government and multilateral urbanization data, and trade and industry-association reporting — rather than an original FOIS field survey. Every statistic cited here is sourced and linked below. Where a figure is a projection or an estimate rather than an audited measurement, we have labelled it as such. FOIS has not surveyed interior design practitioners in this market for this report, and does not represent any number here as an FOIS-original data point.
