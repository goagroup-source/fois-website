AI Adoption in Interior Design

From pre-project visualization to space planning, generative AI has moved from novelty to daily workflow for a growing share of design firms. This briefing tracks how fast adoption is moving, where it concentrates, and what practitioners still don't trust it to do.

11 minRead Time

Executive Summary

AI has crossed from novelty to habit

Houzz's 2026 AI Adoption Report finds that 41% of construction and design firms now use AI for everyday business tasks, up 7 percentage points on the previous year. Among design firms specifically, adoption reached 39%, up 8 points year-on-year — and 97% of professionals surveyed expect AI to meaningfully transform the industry within five years. This is no longer an early-adopter story; it is a mainstream operational shift, and 2026 looks likely to be the year AI stopped being a talking point and became a line item in how design firms plan their week.

Usage is concentrated in a fairly narrow set of tasks. Among design firms, 82% report using AI for pre-project visualization — showing clients a space before it exists — and the share using AI primarily for planning and design work doubled year-on-year to 68%, overtaking other use cases. That pattern is echoed in the broader market: Grand View Research values the global AI-in-interior-design market at roughly $4.0 billion in 2026, projecting growth to $15.0 billion by 2033 at a 20.9% compound annual rate, with cloud-based visualization and space-planning tools leading deployment. Separately, a 1stDibs survey reported that AI use among interior designers roughly tripled year-over-year, suggesting the shift extends beyond production tools into high-end residential design practice.

Trust, however, is lagging usage. Industry-compiled data suggests only around 18% of firms feel AI has reduced their need for professional designers — human judgement is still seen as the differentiator — while concerns about the reliability and accuracy of AI outputs rose from 33% to 37% year-on-year in the Houzz survey. Read together, the data describes augmentation rather than replacement: AI is fast becoming the default first step in a project, but designers remain firmly in the loop on what happens next.

Methodology

How this briefing was built

This briefing synthesises published market research, industry surveys and trade reporting reviewed and contextualised by the FOIS Intelligence editorial team. FOIS has not run its own survey of designers' AI habits; every figure here is attributed to the third-party research firm or publication that produced it, and figures from different surveys are presented side by side rather than merged into a single dataset. Where a source's underlying sample size or methodology was not disclosed in the material we reviewed, we have noted the source only and avoided implying greater precision than the original research supports.
