Hospitality Trends 2026: Designing for the Feeling Economy

Capital is returning to hotel real estate faster than it left. What it is funding has changed — wellness commands the premium, biophilic materiality replaces decorative excess, and the guest experience has become the balance sheet's most closely watched line item.

Sept 2026Published
9 MinReading Time
HospitalitySector
ReportFormat

Executive Summary

Wellness is no longer an amenity. It is a pricing strategy.

Hospitality design in 2026 is being reshaped less by aesthetics than by economics. Hotels that treat wellness, sleep, and sensory design as integrated infrastructure — not spa-floor add-ons — are commanding materially higher average daily rates, and investors are rewarding operators who can prove it. At the same time, capital that fled the sector during the pandemic downturn is returning, but selectively: it is following renovation-first, experience-led assets rather than ground-up supply.

Three forces are converging on the interior. First, a widening ultra-high-net-worth traveller base is pulling branded residences and fashion-house hospitality tie-ups into the mainstream design conversation. Second, Gen Z guests — raised inside always-on digital environments — are paying a premium for engineered disconnection: analogue textures, tech-free floors, and spaces that feel unmistakably physical. Third, an ageing and longevity-focused demographic is pushing wellness programming from a spa-menu line item into a design language that touches lighting, acoustics, materiality, and circulation across the entire property.

Sustainability has quietly moved from marketing copy to underwriting criteria: preservation-first renovations are being explicitly modelled against the embodied carbon of new-build alternatives, and the strongest 2026 case studies are the ones that kept structure and re-imagined everything else.

Analysis

Four design shifts worth underwriting

1. Biophilic materiality over decorative wellness. The spa is no longer where wellness lives — it is dispersed through circadian lighting, acoustic-first material selection, and natural materials in corridors, lobbies, and guestrooms. Properties reporting the strongest ADR premiums treat wellness as a whole-building design brief, not a discrete floor.

2. Preservation as a carbon and cost strategy. Adaptive reuse case studies — most visibly resort renovations that retained existing structure rather than rebuilding — are being cited as achieving a small fraction of the embodied carbon of comparable new construction, while compressing both timeline and capital outlay. Expect embodied-carbon disclosure to become a standard line in hospitality design briefs before 2028.

3. Food and beverage as a design anchor, not an afterthought. With a majority of luxury travellers now naming dining quality as a stay-deciding factor, F&B venues are being designed with the same spatial ambition as lobbies — often as the property's primary local-resident touchpoint, stabilising weekday revenue in gateway cities.

4. Multigenerational and longevity design. Kids' programming quality now materially influences family booking decisions, while a parallel push toward longevity and Blue Zone-inspired wellness is reshaping amenity design for older travellers. The interior is being asked to serve both ends of the age spectrum inside the same footprint.

The properties winning capital in 2026 are not the ones with the newest floor plates — they are the ones that can prove, in dollars, what their design decisions do to a guest's nervous system.

How this report was built

This report synthesises published research and transaction data from hospitality real estate advisors, design consultancies, and hospitality trade press — cross-referenced for consistency and cited individually below. Where a figure originates from a named third-party survey or dataset, that source is credited rather than presented as FOIS's own fieldwork.

Statistics reflect the most recent publicly available data as of September 2026 and should be read as directional industry signal rather than property-level guarantees.
