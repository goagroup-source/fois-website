In Conversation: Designing for Ageing Populations

FOIS Intelligence · 2026

As multigenerational households become more common across the markets FOIS Intelligence tracks, we spoke with a residential design practitioner about what "ageing in place" actually demands of an interior.

FOIS Intelligence

What's the biggest misconception clients have about designing for older residents?

That it means removing personality from a home. The best examples we've seen do the opposite — they solve for grip, lighting and step-free movement so quietly that a visitor wouldn't notice, and spend the visible budget on the things that make a home feel like a home.

"Accessible design that looks institutional has already failed at half its job."

FOIS Intelligence

Where are you seeing the most interesting material innovation for this segment?

Flooring, honestly. Slip-resistant finishes that don't read as clinical have improved enormously in the last two years — that's a small technical shift with an outsized effect on how a space feels to live in every day.

FOIS Intelligence

What should the industry be doing differently?

Designing for this earlier. Right now most of this thinking happens in a renovation, reactively, after a health event. It should be a standard design consideration from the first residential brief, the same way daylighting or storage already is.
