The Future Workplace Report

As hybrid work moves into its fifth year, the office is being rebuilt as a destination for collaboration and technology, not a row of desks. This briefing synthesises 2026's occupancy, design and AI-adoption data to map where workplace design is heading next.

Executive Summary

The office is filling back up — on different terms

Average global office utilization climbed to 53% in 2026, up sharply from 38% in 2024 and 35% in 2023, according to CBRE's analysis of more than 300 million square feet of client office portfolios. The story behind that number is not a simple return to 2019 — it is a re-sorting of what the office is for. CBRE finds that 68% of employees now name collaboration, not routine desk work, as their primary reason for coming in, reinforcing a shift toward offices designed as "destinations" rather than workstation inventory.

Yet supply hasn't caught up with that new demand. Gensler's 2026 Global Workplace Survey, drawn from more than 16,000 office workers across 16 countries, finds that 66% of employees regularly adapt to or work around poor space design — using meeting rooms for solo focus work, taking calls in hallways, or holding meetings at their own desks because no suitable room is free. These are not edge cases; they are now majority behaviours, and they point directly at where interior designers and workplace strategists have room to add value.

Layered onto this is a technology shift: roughly 30% of employees in the Gensler survey qualify as "AI power users," and this group shows measurably different office behaviour — coming in more often for collaboration and technology access, and spending more time learning and connecting with colleagues. At the same time, JLL's 2026 US construction outlook flags rising material costs and persistent labour constraints, meaning the workplaces designers are being asked to reshape must do more with tighter capital. Together, these signals describe a workplace design brief for the second half of 2026 that is less about square footage and more about precision: fewer, better-considered spaces built around how people actually collaborate.

Methodology

How this briefing was built

This briefing synthesises published market research, occupancy data and trade reporting from established research firms and industry publications, reviewed and contextualised by the FOIS Intelligence editorial team. FOIS did not conduct an independent workplace survey for this report; every statistic is drawn from, and attributed to, the third-party sources listed below. Where figures come from different survey samples or methodologies, we have noted the source alongside each statistic rather than blending them into a single dataset.
