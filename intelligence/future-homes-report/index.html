The Future Homes Report

Smaller footprints, multigenerational households, home-office integration, connected systems and wellness features are no longer separate trends — together, they are redefining what a home is designed to do.

Executive Summary

The home is being re-programmed

The residential brief has quietly rewritten itself over the last decade. Affordability pressure is shrinking the average footprint even as more generations share it; a majority of employed people now do at least some work from home, turning a spare corner into functional infrastructure; and a home's systems and surfaces are increasingly expected to actively support health rather than simply shelter it.

None of these shifts is hypothetical. They show up in Census-adjacent housing data, in labour-market surveys, and in the balance sheets of the smart-home and wellness-real-estate industries. What is new is how tightly they interact: a smaller home has to work harder per square foot, which is exactly why home offices, adaptable guest/elder suites, and integrated technology are being designed in from the start rather than retrofitted.

This report sets out the five forces we see doing the most to reshape residential design briefs today, with the market and demographic data behind each one.

The Five Forces

What's actually reshaping the brief

Smaller, Harder-Working Footprints

New US single-family homes have trended smaller since 2015 as affordability pressure pushes buyers toward efficient, well-planned layouts over sheer square footage — placing a premium on flexible, multi-use rooms.

Multigenerational Living

The population living in multigenerational US households has roughly quadrupled since 1971, now spanning nearly a fifth of the country — driving demand for semi-private suites, dual kitchens and adaptable ground-floor bedrooms.

Home-Office Integration

Roughly 58% of employed Americans report having the opportunity to work from home at least one day a week, making a dedicated, acoustically separated workspace a default line item rather than an afterthought.

Smart Home Technology

Connected lighting, climate, security and energy systems are moving from optional upgrade to baseline expectation, with the global smart home market forecast to grow more than fivefold between 2025 and 2033.

Wellness Features

Air quality, circadian lighting, water quality and biophilic design are shifting from wellness-brand amenity to mainstream residential spec, tracked by a wellness real estate market forecast to nearly double by 2029.

Methodology
